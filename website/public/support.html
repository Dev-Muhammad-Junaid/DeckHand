What Pixel Hand is

Pixel Hand turns an iPhone or iPad into a remote for your Mac: a precision trackpad, a keyboard, a live view of the Mac’s screen, captures, and shortcuts that follow the app in front.

It comes in two parts:

  • The iPhone and iPad app (iOS and iPadOS 17.4 or later), from the App Store.
  • The free Mac app (macOS 14 Sonoma or later), downloaded from pixelhand.app. It lives in the menu bar and does the work on the Mac side.

Both devices need to be on the same local network, such as your home or office Wi-Fi. They connect to each other directly; nothing goes through our servers.

Questions

My iPhone or iPad can’t find my Mac

  • Make sure the Mac app is running: its icon should be in the menu bar.
  • Check that both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network. Guest networks and some office networks keep devices apart, so try another network if you can.
  • On the iPhone or iPad, open Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network and turn on Pixel Hand. On macOS 15 or later, check the same on the Mac under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network.
  • Turn off any VPN on either device, then quit and reopen both apps.

How do I approve my iPhone or iPad on the Mac?

The first time a device connects, the Mac asks whether to allow it, with a notification and in the Pixel Hand menu-bar panel. Click Allow to connect, or Deny to turn it away. Nothing is shared until you allow it.

Approvals last until the Mac app quits. After you reopen it, the Mac asks once more. That’s deliberate: a device is never trusted silently.

The pointer or keyboard doesn’t do anything on the Mac

The Mac app needs Accessibility permission to move the pointer, press keys, and read the menu shortcuts of the app in front. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, turn on Pixel Hand, then quit and reopen the Mac app. If it’s already on but still doesn’t work (this can happen after an update), remove Pixel Hand from the list with the minus button and add it again.

The live view or captures are blank

Those need Screen Recording permission. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording (called Screen Recording on older versions of macOS), turn on Pixel Hand, then quit and reopen the Mac app. The live view and captures go only to the devices you’ve approved.

How do I turn off crash reports?

The apps send anonymous crash and performance reports so we can fix problems. They never include your screen, what you type, or your captures. To turn them off, open Settings → Privacy in the iPhone or iPad app and switch off Share crash reports, or untick Share crash reports at the bottom of the Mac app’s menu-bar panel. Each device has its own switch. The privacy policy has the details.

How do I update the Mac app?

It checks for updates by itself. To check now, open its menu-bar panel and choose Check for Updates.
